So here it is. Beyond the test articles about pringles men and Juan and lord knows whatever else was put on here during the testing phase, this website is finally functional. I think it is worth giving a little note on how we ended up here, and what this is for.

Perhaps the easiest way is just describe two scenes.

**The first scene** being drawn here is of a student, a lonely burdgeoning academic with her candlelit desk, mokka pot coffee perfuming her dorm, and Google Docs open. There's a paper due in a week—plenty of time—so she takes her time. She writes about history, politics, love, the future, and all of her subliminal thoughts begin to meander on the page in front of her.

After a week, she's done. The paper is submitted, the professor gives an astounding mark, and for a brief period she feels that the whole week behind her genuinely was an enjoyable time, and that paper was *genuinely fun to write*. 

And then its gone. 

The joy, the product, those meandering thoughts forever imprisoned in cloud storage.

**The second scene** is probably also a familiar one. A few students this time, not lonely and romantic, but animated by good spirits (I will let you decide what "spirits" mean in this context). The candlelight they're using comes from the great big flame in the sky. One within the group poses a question: "Do you think Foucault would like ChatGPT?" Someone takes a gamble: "Yeah he definitely would" A disagreement arises: "To be honest I feel like he would see it as a bit of a panopticon" The gambler: "Sure but he'd see it as a means to the realization of the individual" The disagreer: "But he'd hate the ethics of it"

And so it goes, for hours on end. Some in the group called themselves Foucaultian. Others have never read a word of Foucault, and they all debate just the same. But eventually the topic of conversation changes. Someone has to run to catch the bus home, others slowly part ways and the group dissapates, and so too does the conversation, and that beautiful Hegelian Foucaultian-AI dialectic. 

In some way, our goal, here at *Students Unbridled,* is to change the fate of the dissapointed high-spirited academic and the foucaultian masterminds. What if that paper didnt have to just live in the clouds, and that conversation had a place where others could join in on the conversation? That is what this is. 

As a serial student myself, I recognize how often it is our works go to waste, and thoughts that might have created something of value are easily disregarded. "Our brains are good at creating ideas, just not at remembering them" said someone whose name I genuinely can't remember. I want to encourage everyone to write more. To place ideas—no matter how seemingly unimportant—onto a sheet of paper. It is so easy to forget ideas, or to think that my mind is not capable of coming up with great thoughts, that I can't change the world. But it always starts small. A word, sentence, drawing, or conversation. By putting it on a paper you've already put the fish that was flopping on land into a little bowl of water. But it needs the sea.

That's when you need to take that piece of work and publish it online. Unafraid of what others might say. Unafraid of the rigorous and pretentious standards imposed by the gatekeepers of intellect. You are capable of being free, of letting the world read your half-assed thoughts. Indeed, that is when they might be at their most valuable.

So feel free to live here for a bit. Have a read around—there might not be much for a while—but we're working on it. Reach out to us with your shower thoughts. **Here is the task for you:** Dig through your file cabinet (digital or not), and see what you've got. You might come across a miniature polemic you wrote for an old english class. Rewrite it. Make it structured and argumentative. Then send it our way. Who knows, it may be on a front cover soon.
