About Students Unbridled

Students Unbridled is an independent, student-run publication for essays that take a position and defend it. We publish work centered on politics and public life, but we're open to any subject a writer can make a real argument about—culture, science, ethics, and beyond. We hold the view that all things worldly are political, all things relate to each other, and all things must be deconstructed.

We are not affiliated with any single class, department, or student organization. Anyone—student or not—is welcome to submit.

Broad Editorial Theme

The publishers of this paper take an explicitly left-wing approach to society and politics. Therefore, we encourage any pieces that develop on this theme. The point, however, is not to create a dogmatic journal, but to open a space for any work students may have done on politics, economy, society, culture, etc. that, rather than depreciating in the bowels of our own insecurities as students, may be granted the space to live out there on the vast interwebs, granted a trial in the court of public opinion, allowing your voice to reach the world.

We thus welcome many genres, and will take many efforts to provide adequate space for varying themes, clearly delineated so.

Two formats, clearly labeled

Every essay we publish is labeled as one of two formats, so readers always know what kind of writing they're getting.

Academic

Formal essays that follow academic conventions: a clear thesis, structured argument, and evidence. Citations are welcome but not required if the piece isn't research-based. Think coursework-quality argumentative or analytical writing, that final research paper sitting in your professor’s archive.

Non-Academic

Op-eds, personal essays, reported pieces, satire, criticism—looser in form, still built around a real point of view. Conversational tone is acceptable, and perhaps encouraged.

Categories

Essays submitted to this journal will be labeled by theme as well. The goal is to allow contributors to publish works already building on a particular conversation, and to allow readers to intentionally eavesdrop on a conversation that interests them.

What we're looking for

  • Clear, defensible point of views.
  • Any takes welcome—Hot, Mild, or Minty*
  • Original work, not previously published elsewhere (No legal issues pls)
  • Pieces of any size welcome. Though be wary, if your piece is ~10k+ words, we we still read it, but consider whether an alternate publication or journal fits your project.
  • Civil, good-faith argument. We welcome disagreement, not personal attacks

Editorial process

Our editorial team reviews submissions before publication. We will then email you if your piece has been chosen for review, and we will also notify you when the editorial process will begin. We may suggest edits for clarity or length.

  • We do not publish anonymously without discussing it with the author first.
  • Any published piece is welcome to changes—The first take is always the best take.
  • We’ll never alter a piece without your knowledge—The point is to remain as unbridled as possible
  • We'll never publish a piece without your knowledge.
  • Currently, submissions to students unbridled cannot include images or stylized text. I realize this is a massive hurdle and has to do with the fact that this website is mostly hand-codded and I am not great at coding. This issue will hopefully be resolved in the coming weeks. For now, remember that writers are welcome to update their papers when new features are added!

* The editors of this paper concur with Alex O'Conner's view that Minty is the opposite of Spicy. If you disagree, you can write a piece about it
